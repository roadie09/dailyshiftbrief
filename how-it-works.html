  3. Ask the two questions. The crew answers out loud, not with a nod.

Shift-Start Safety Briefing: Unguarded Conveyor Roller

A fingertip amputation at a motor vehicle parts plant in Alabama · Shift-start briefing · 10-15 minutes

Built from the OSHA public record. Review with your site's competent person before use. This briefing does not replace training required by specific OSHA standards. Employer name withheld. Incident facts are from the employer's report to OSHA.

What went wrong

The employer reported to OSHA that on February 6, 2025, at a motor vehicle parts plant in Alabama, an employee was troubleshooting a conveyor that was sticking. His glove was pulled into the conveyor roller. He suffered a fingertip amputation.

The employer reported to OSHA that the machine was not guarded at the time.

The control that would have stopped it

A guard at the conveyor roller — a fixed barrier that keeps hands, gloves, and clothing out of the in-running nip point where the roller turns. The employer reported to OSHA that the machine was not guarded.

Guarding is the primary control. If a conveyor is sticking and the work takes someone up to a roller, the machine must also be shut down, locked out, and checked so it cannot start before a hand or glove goes near the nip point.

From January 1 through November 30, 2025, federal OSHA recorded 612 severe injuries in fabricated metal and transportation equipment manufacturing (NAICS 332 and 336). Two hundred seventy-six of those reports involved an amputation. Seventy reports — the same event type as this incident — were contact with running powered equipment during maintenance, cleaning, or testing.

Checks for this shift

  1. Before you start, walk the conveyors you will run or service. If a roller, pulley, belt, chain, or sprocket nip point is open, do not operate or troubleshoot it. Report it and get the guard back in place.
  2. If a conveyor is sticking, do not reach in. Do not use a glove, rag, or tool to free it while the conveyor can still move. Shut it down, lock and tag it, and verify it will not start before you put a hand near a roller.
  3. A glove can be pulled into a nip point, as the employer reported happened here. That is why the guard has to be in place during operation, and why lockout comes first when the work takes you to the roller.
  4. Confirm you know where the emergency stop is on each conveyor you will work this shift, and that you can reach it.

Discussion questions

  1. On the conveyors you will run or service this shift, where is a roller, pulley, belt, or chain nip point that is unguarded or easy to reach?
  2. If a conveyor sticks this shift, what is the exact sequence we use before anyone puts a hand, glove, or tool near a roller?
